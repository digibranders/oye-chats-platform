## Central Idea

A business's website should do more than sit there — it should talk to every visitor, know its own content well enough to actually help them, quietly figure out who's worth a salesperson's time, and hand that person to the business's own team at exactly the right moment.

One sentence: **OyeChats turns a static website into an active, knowledgeable, self-qualifying front door for the business.**

---

## The Old World

A visitor arrives on a business's website with a question. They read, they scan, they search — and if the answer isn't obviously placed, they leave, or they email/call and wait. Nothing about that visit is remembered: not what they were interested in, not how close they were to becoming a customer, not that they were ever there at all. The website is a brochure, not a participant.

*(This is a narrative framing of the problem the product solves, not a sourced market statistic — no inspected material contains a quantified figure for this. Keep it as a scene-setting contrast, not a claim with a number attached.)*

## The Turning Point

The business points OyeChats at its own website. In minutes, the platform has read every page, every brochure, every document — and it hasn't just learned facts, it has learned how the business talks and what it looks like, pulling brand tone and colors directly from the site itself. One line of code later, the website is no longer static. **[T2 — `docs/oyechats-technical-story.md` Part 4.2 Step 3]**

## The New Experience

A visitor asks a question. The answer comes back immediately, in the business's own voice, sourced only from the business's own real content — never a guess, never borrowed from the open internet. If the AI genuinely doesn't know, it says so honestly instead of inventing something plausible. **[T2 — Part 6.6]**

Behind that one reply, the conversation is quietly being scored against a real sales-qualification framework — no interrogation, no chip-tapping gauntlet by default, just a silent read on how ready this visitor is. **[T2 — Part 7, Part 7.1]** The moment that visitor crosses into "worth a human's time," the business's team is notified everywhere they might be looking. **[T2 — Part 7.4, Part 9.6]**

If the visitor wants a person, one shows up — routed to whoever's actually free, arriving already holding the full conversation, the visitor's journey, and their qualification score, so nobody starts cold. **[T2 — Part 9.7]**

## Business Value

For a CEO, CMO, or business owner, the value is not "we have an AI chatbot" — it's:

- **Nothing falls through the cracks.** Every conversation becomes a real, enriched lead record, not a forgotten browser tab. **[T2 — Part 8]**
- **The team's time goes to the right visitors.** Qualification happens automatically, in the background, before a human ever gets involved. **[T2 — Part 7]**
- **Setup cost is near zero.** No FAQ to hand-write, no developer required to install, no ongoing manual upkeep — the knowledge base re-checks itself on a schedule. **[T2 — Part 5.13, Part 4.2]**
- **Churn isn't catastrophic.** If a subscription lapses, knowledge is paused, not deleted — reactivating restores everything exactly as it was. **[T2 — Part 11]**

## Emotional Journey

The intended emotional arc for someone encountering this story:

1. **Recognition** — "yes, that's what my website does right now: nothing, when someone has a question."
2. **Relief** — "it reads *my* site, in ten minutes, without a developer."
3. **Confidence** — "it won't make things up — it tells the truth or says it doesn't know."
4. **Anticipation** — "it's already telling me which visitors are worth calling."
5. **Reassurance** — "a real person still closes the deal; the AI just clears the way."
6. **Trust** — "even if I stop paying for a bit, I don't lose my work."

This progression is derived directly from the sequence of capabilities in the product story (Parts 4 → 6 → 7 → 9 → 11) — it is not an invented emotional framework layered on top.

## Differentiation

What the approved material actually supports as differentiating (not generic AI-chatbot claims):

- **Refuses honestly instead of hallucinating** — a relevance gate blocks answering from irrelevant material, and a background auditor continuously checks whether generated claims were actually supported. **[T2 — Part 6.6, Part 6.10]**
- **Qualification chips are off by default** — a deliberate choice against the industry pattern of mid-chat interrogation, because "the market leaders default them off too." **[T2 — Part 7.1, direct quote from source]**
- **Reversible churn** — knowledge is deactivated, never deleted, on lapse. **[T2 — Part 11]**
- **Brand-matched from day one** — colors and tone extracted from the business's own site during setup, not manually configured. **[T2 — Part 4.2 Step 3, Step 5]**

Do not claim differentiation on anything not listed above (e.g., speed-of-setup superlatives, model-quality superlatives, or comparisons to named competitors) — no such comparative claims exist in the source material.

## Messages To Avoid

- **Do not claim the AI replaces the sales/support team.** The product's own architecture is built around human handoff — see `OYECHATS_MASTER_KNOWLEDGE.md` Section 10. Frame the AI as a qualifier/first-responder, never a replacement.
- **Do not cite any conversion-rate, ROI, or revenue-lift number.** No inspected source contains one.
- **Do not claim USD/multi-currency billing as live.** INR via Razorpay is the sole live rail; USD pricing exists in configuration but is staged behind a flag. **[T2 — root `CLAUDE.md` Tech Stack table]**
- **Do not depict visitors being asked a rapid-fire sequence of qualifying questions.** Interactive qualification chips ship off by default; the default experience is silent background scoring. **[T2 — Part 7.1]**
- **Do not invent customer names, logos, testimonials, or case studies.** None exist in any inspected source.
- **Do not use generic AI-hype language** ("revolutionary," "game-changing," "cutting-edge") — the approved brand tone is plain-spoken and confident, not hype-adjective-stacked. **[T3 — `docs/oyechats-marketing-story.md`]**
