# OyeChats — Marketing Video Style Prompt

*This is the locked visual-style prompt for OyeChats marketing videos. Use it as-is when generating video content. It is a creative-direction/style spec, not a product-fact document — it carries no evidence tags and is not part of this package's [T1]/[T2]/[VERIFY] discipline.*

---

Create a premium cinematic 3D technical explainer in the visual language of a high-end product film, architectural miniature, luxury paper engineering, and sophisticated enterprise technology documentary.

The core visual concept is **"a digital system physically brought to life."** Every interface, workflow, data point, website, conversation, AI process, and user journey should feel tangible and physically constructed inside a meticulously designed miniature environment.

The result must feel intelligent, premium, engineered, calm, tactile, cinematic and highly intentional — never like a generic AI SaaS advertisement.

## 1. GLOBAL VISUAL LANGUAGE

Build the entire world as a beautifully engineered miniature physical system.

Digital interfaces should not appear as ordinary flat screenshots floating in space. Instead, transform them into tangible objects:

* layered interface cards
* raised UI panels
* physical navigation tabs
* recessed controls
* embossed labels
* miniature displays
* tactile buttons
* stacked paper sheets
* architectural system diagrams
* physical data blocks
* miniature workstations
* small devices
* connected modules
* sculptural AI components
* physical chat windows
* cards representing users, conversations, data, intent and outcomes

Every component must have believable physical depth, thickness, edge detail, contact shadows and material response.

The visual metaphor should consistently communicate:

**software becoming physical infrastructure.**

---

## 2. COLOR SYSTEM

Use a sophisticated warm editorial palette.

Primary colors:

* warm ivory
* cream
* parchment
* beige
* taupe
* muted stone
* charcoal
* deep navy-black

Suggested approximate palette:

#F0EFEA
#E8E5DE
#D8D3C9
#C8BEB0
#AAA093
#827569
#4C423B
#111319

Use accent colors only when they communicate meaning.

Accent colors:

* muted gold / amber = intelligence, activation, AI processing
* warm orange = location, movement, context
* restrained red = warning, failure, blocked state
* muted blue = technical/system information
* muted green = successful state / resolution

Maintain a neutral environment with one dominant accent at a time.

Never use:

* neon purple
* neon cyan
* rainbow gradients
* excessive blue
* excessive purple
* cyberpunk colors
* glowing gradients everywhere

Color should create hierarchy, not decoration.

---

## 3. ENVIRONMENT

Build scenes inside premium miniature environments resembling a hybrid of:

* architectural model
* luxury product studio
* engineering workbench
* editorial design table
* physical information system

Use large amounts of negative space.

Surfaces should include:

* matte ivory paper
* slightly textured cardstock
* soft molded plastic
* matte ceramic
* brushed dark metal
* subtle natural wood
* muted technical materials

Materials must feel real.

Paper should have slight fiber and edge variation.

Plastic should have subtle roughness.

Metal should show restrained reflections.

Objects must interact naturally with surfaces.

---

## 4. LIGHTING

Use premium product-photography lighting.

Primary light:

Large, soft, diffused studio source positioned from above and slightly to one side.

Lighting characteristics:

* soft broad shadows
* gentle gradients across surfaces
* warm-neutral illumination
* subtle edge highlights
* realistic contact shadows
* restrained reflections
* soft ambient fill

Avoid harsh theatrical lighting except when intentionally entering a dark technology scene.

Objects should always feel grounded.

Every raised card, button, sheet and component must cast a believable shadow.

---

## 5. CAMERA LANGUAGE

Use sophisticated macro product cinematography.

Camera movements should include:

* slow push-ins
* gentle dolly movements
* controlled lateral slides
* subtle 3/4 orbits
* macro reveals
* slow overhead movements
* architectural top-down shots
* restrained perspective changes
* smooth rack-focus transitions

Camera movement should feel physically operated and expensive.

Never use:

* chaotic handheld motion
* rapid spinning
* aggressive zooms
* random camera shaking
* excessive whip pans
* hyperactive transitions

Use realistic lens behavior and cinematic depth of field.

Foreground objects may be extremely sharp while background elements gradually soften.

The viewer should feel as though a real macro cinema camera is filming a miniature physical system.

---

## 6. UI AS PHYSICAL OBJECTS

Treat every UI screenshot or interface as a physical object.

A static dashboard should never simply sit on screen.

It can:

* slide into the environment
* rise from a surface
* unfold like a technical blueprint
* split into layers
* stack with other panels
* rotate slightly
* reveal hidden modules
* expand specific components
* rearrange itself
* physically connect to other systems
* transform into a new scene

The UI should retain the original visual design while acquiring physical depth.

Use realistic:

* bevels
* thickness
* spacing
* edge shadows
* reflections
* surface texture
* depth between layers

The result should feel like a physical product prototype of a digital interface.

---

## 7. ENHANCED UI ANIMATION

Introduce significantly more UI animation than a traditional static explainer, while keeping every motion elegant and physically believable.

Use:

### Layered assembly

A UI card appears as an empty physical panel.

Then:

1. frame assembles
2. navigation rises into place
3. content cards slide in
4. labels appear
5. controls activate
6. data begins moving

### Micro-interactions

Buttons can:

* depress slightly
* create a small shadow shift
* illuminate subtly
* trigger a connected component
* update a status indicator

Hover states should physically lift a component by a tiny amount.

### Data motion

Show information moving through the interface as subtle illuminated signals.

Example:

**Visitor → Context → AI → Knowledge → Response**

A small warm illuminated signal travels between components.

### Dynamic metrics

Numbers can update naturally:

128 → 129 → 130

Charts can grow physically.

Progress bars can extend.

Indicators can change state.

All animation should be smooth and controlled.

### Chat animation

Chat interfaces should feel alive:

* cursor enters
* text types naturally
* typing indicator activates
* message card rises
* response appears
* AI state changes
* conversation resolves

Message bubbles may physically emerge from the screen as small layered cards.

---

## 8. SCREENSHOT TRANSFORMATION

When using a real product screenshot, do NOT simply display the screenshot for several seconds.

Treat the screenshot as an entry point into the physical world.

Example progression:

Full dashboard
→ slow camera push-in
→ target component becomes active
→ component lifts from the dashboard
→ surrounding interface remains behind
→ component expands into a physical scene
→ camera travels into it
→ new environment begins

The transition should make the viewer feel as though they are entering the interface itself.

---

## 9. UI EXPLODED-VIEW ANIMATION

Use architectural exploded-view compositions.

A dashboard can separate into layers:

Navigation
→ Dashboard
→ Conversations
→ Analytics
→ User context
→ AI system

These components slowly lift apart, revealing relationships between them.

Thin physical connectors can extend between modules.

This should resemble a premium engineering model rather than a generic motion graphic.

---

## 10. PHYSICAL INTERACTION

Give the UI weight and physicality.

Elements may:

* slide
* lift
* press
* rotate
* unfold
* stack
* align
* lock into place
* magnetically connect
* separate
* expand
* collapse

Movement should respect inertia.

No exaggerated bouncing.

No elastic cartoon motion.

No weightless floating.

---

## 11. TYPOGRAPHY

Use a modern enterprise/editorial sans-serif.

Typography should be:

* clean
* restrained
* precise
* technical
* dark charcoal or muted brown
* carefully aligned

Use:

* architectural labels
* concise headings
* small annotations
* system names
* short captions
* component identifiers
* technical metadata

Typography can appear printed, embossed, engraved or subtly integrated into surfaces.

Avoid huge marketing headlines dominating the screen.

Avoid excessive text.

Every label should feel informative.

---

## 12. DIAGRAMS AND SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

Represent complex technology visually through physical diagrams.

Use:

* thin lines
* nodes
* connectors
* modular blocks
* grids
* timelines
* relationship paths
* layered cards
* system labels
* physical data channels

Animate connections progressively.

Example:

Visitor
↓
Website
↓
Chat
↓
Context
↓
AI
↓
Knowledge
↓
Response
↓
Resolution

The viewer should understand the system visually before reading any explanation.

---

## 13. DATA VISUALIZATION

Keep charts minimal and editorial.

Use:

* restrained bar charts
* subtle line graphs
* small indicators
* minimal timelines
* compact metrics
* physical data cards

Data should animate through physical transformation rather than flashy 2D effects.

Example:

A bar physically rises from a panel.

A percentage wheel rotates subtly.

A small metric card changes from neutral to active.

---

## 14. CHARACTERS

Use premium stylized 3D corporate miniature characters.

Characters should have:

* realistic proportions
* clean facial geometry
* professional clothing
* understated expressions
* refined materials
* subtle natural movement

They should feel like beautifully designed 3D figurines inside the miniature environment.

Avoid:

* cartoon exaggeration
* oversized heads
* exaggerated facial expressions
* childish animation
* hyper-realistic uncanny humans

Human interaction should remain subtle and professional.

---

## 15. CHAT AND CONVERSATION AS A RECURRING VISUAL MOTIF

For an intelligent communication product, use the conversation/message element as a recurring visual symbol.

A physical message bubble can:

* appear
* illuminate
* move between systems
* become a user profile
* transform into a data card
* connect with AI
* become a response
* move toward an operator
* finally become a resolved interaction

This creates visual continuity across the entire film.

---

## 16. DARK "INSIDE THE MACHINE" SCENES

Occasionally transition from the warm physical world into a dark technical environment.

Use:

* deep navy-black
* dark graphite
* restrained gold/blue light
* subtle circuitry
* structured data nodes
* technical components
* geometric architecture
* controlled glowing connections

The dark scenes represent:

**entering the intelligence layer / backend / AI processing core.**

Keep them elegant.

Do not turn them into cyberpunk.

---

## 17. ACCENT OBJECTS

Use a mostly neutral composition with one visual focal point.

Examples:

* one red warning indicator
* one glowing gold AI component
* one orange map marker
* one green resolution state
* one blue technical node

The focal object should attract attention naturally through contrast and subtle illumination.

---

## 18. TRANSITIONS

Avoid generic transition effects.

Use object-driven conceptual transitions.

Examples:

A card slides into another card.

A diagram folds into a new architecture.

A message bubble becomes a data node.

A dashboard component becomes a physical workstation.

A stack of paper becomes a software architecture.

A camera pushes through a UI panel and enters the next environment.

A data path continues from one scene into the following scene.

The final frame of one scene should often become the first physical element of the next.

---

## 19. MOTION PHYSICS

All animation must feel physically plausible.

Use:

* realistic inertia
* subtle acceleration/deceleration
* believable mass
* contact with surfaces
* natural object overlap
* realistic shadows during movement

Avoid weightless floating UI.

Avoid random bounce.

Avoid excessive spring physics.

Avoid animation that looks like standard web motion graphics.

---

## 20. DEPTH AND COMPOSITION

Create strong foreground, midground and background layering.

Use:

* foreground object
* primary subject
* supporting system components
* softly blurred background environment

Maintain significant negative space.

Do not overcrowd scenes.

Each shot should have one clear visual idea.

---

## 21. VISUAL RHYTHM

Use an intelligent pacing structure.

Establishing shot:
3–6 seconds

Detail shot:
2–4 seconds

Transformation:
3–6 seconds

System architecture:
3–7 seconds

Character interaction:
3–6 seconds

Macro reveal:
2–5 seconds

Do not cut constantly.

Allow the viewer to understand each visual metaphor.

The pacing should feel cinematic and confident, not social-media frantic.

---

## 22. NARRATIVE VISUAL FLOW

Prefer a visual progression such as:

Problem
→ website
→ visitor
→ interaction
→ context
→ data
→ AI understanding
→ routing
→ knowledge
→ response
→ human/AI assistance
→ resolution
→ complete intelligent system

The entire film should feel like one connected machine.

Every scene should naturally explain why the next scene exists.

---

## 23. PREMIUM QUALITY RULES

Every frame must satisfy these principles:

1. Realistic physical depth.
2. Believable material behavior.
3. Soft cinematic lighting.
4. Controlled camera movement.
5. Restrained color palette.
6. One clear focal point.
7. Purposeful animation.
8. Minimal typography.
9. Strong negative space.
10. Consistent physical world.
11. Seamless conceptual transitions.
12. No visual clutter.

The video should look like it was created by a premium product-design studio rather than generated from generic AI templates.

---

## 24. ABSOLUTE "DO NOT" RULES

Do not use:

* generic SaaS landing-page aesthetics
* flat floating screenshots
* neon purple backgrounds
* excessive gradients
* holographic dashboards
* glassmorphism
* excessive particle effects
* cyberpunk environments
* exaggerated lens flares
* flashy transitions
* cartoon UI
* oversized marketing text
* stock footage
* generic corporate B-roll
* unrealistic floating cards
* chaotic camera movement
* excessive glowing elements
* rainbow color palettes
* excessive motion for its own sake

Do not make everything animated.

Animation should direct attention and communicate meaning.

---

## 25. FINAL ART DIRECTION

The finished video should feel like:

**a luxury product film for an invisible digital machine that has been physically engineered and placed on a designer's worktable.**

Imagine:

**Apple product cinematography**
+
**architectural model photography**
+
**luxury paper engineering**
+
**premium 3D animation**
+
**enterprise systems visualization**
+
**editorial technical documentary**

The final result must communicate:

**intelligent technology made tangible.**

Every static screenshot should have the potential to become an animated physical object.

Every interaction should have weight.

Every transition should communicate meaning.

Every color should have a purpose.

Every camera movement should feel deliberate.

Every scene should feel like part of the same miniature physical universe.

The goal is not to make a flashy AI advertisement.

The goal is to make a **cinematic visual explanation of a sophisticated digital system — tactile, intelligent, premium, physical, and memorable.**
