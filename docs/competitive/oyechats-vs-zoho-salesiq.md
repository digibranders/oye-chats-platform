*Created 2026-08-30. This is the single source for every OyeChats-vs-Zoho-SalesIQ claim
we publish — the `/compare/oyechats-vs-zoho-salesiq` page on the marketing site, sales
decks, and any ad copy. **Marketing copy is derived from this file, never written
independently of it.** If a claim is not in this file, it does not ship.*

**Why this comparison matters more than the other six.** Zoho is an Indian company with
overwhelming mindshare among Indian SMBs, which is our primary ICP. For a large share of
our target buyers, SalesIQ is not "a competitor we might be evaluated against" — it is
the tool they are already paying for, bundled into a suite they already trust, sold by a
brand they already know. Every other comparison page is a keyword play. This one is the
actual sales objection.

## 1. Claim posture — the rules this page is written under

These are not stylistic preferences. They are the reason this page is legally and
editorially safe, and they carry forward the rules already stated at the top of
`oyechats-website-v2/src/lib/compare.ts`.

1. **No live competitor pricing numbers.** We characterise SalesIQ's pricing *model*
   (per-operator tiers, free entry tier, discounted annually, cheaper inside the wider
   Zoho suite) and never quote a figure. Rival pricing changes without notice; a stale
   number on our site is both a credibility loss and an unnecessary legal exposure. Our
   *own* numbers are fine — they are ours, they are already public, and we control them.
2. **No invented benchmarks.** No "3x more qualified leads", no "40% faster setup", no
   accuracy percentages. `OYECHATS_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` already marks every conversion,
   ROI or revenue figure as **NOT SUPPORTED — do not use**, and that ruling applies here
   without exception. **[DO NOT PUBLISH]**
3. **No named customers or testimonials.** None exist in any inspected source. **[DO NOT PUBLISH]**
4. **SalesIQ gets a genuine, prominent "choose them instead" section**, written to be
   actually persuasive rather than a strawman. There are real buyers for whom SalesIQ is
   the correct answer, and saying so is better SEO under Google's helpful-content system,
   better for trust, and safer than a one-sided grid.
5. **Our own gaps are stated, not hidden.** Specifically: we have no native Zoho CRM
   integration, our operator-seat count is lower at entry, and our suite surface is a
   fraction of Zoho One's. A buyer who discovers an omission after signup churns anyway.
6. **Trademark hygiene.** "Zoho" and "Zoho SalesIQ" are trademarks of Zoho Corporation.
   We use the names nominatively to identify the product being compared, we use a letter
   monogram rather than their logo (existing `monogram` convention in `compare.ts`), and
   we do not imply endorsement, affiliation or partnership.

## 2. What each product actually is

### OyeChats
An AI chatbot platform where a business points the product at its own content, gets a
private knowledge base, and embeds a widget with one script tag. Answers are generated by
RAG grounded in that content; every conversation is silently scored for sales-readiness;
a human can take over the same thread at any point. **[T2]** — root `CLAUDE.md`.

### Zoho SalesIQ
A live chat, visitor-tracking and engagement product inside the Zoho suite. Its centre of
gravity is human live chat plus visitor intelligence, with a bot builder (Zobot) layered
on, and its defining strength is native integration with Zoho CRM, Zoho Desk and the rest
of the Zoho ecosystem. **[CAT]**

**The one-sentence framing** — this is the sentence the whole page hangs on:

> SalesIQ is live chat that has added AI. OyeChats is an AI agent that has added live chat.

That is a difference in what each product's *defaults* optimise for, not a claim that
either one lacks a checkbox. It is honest, it is defensible, and it is the actual
distinction a buyer feels in week one.

## 3. The comparison axes

These map onto `FEATURE_AXES` in `compare.ts`, plus four axes that only this page carries.
`edge` is judged from our perspective but marks `them` and `tie` honestly.

### 3.1 Grounded answers from your own content — *edge: oye*

**OyeChats.** Hybrid retrieval — pgvector semantic search over 768-dim
`gemini-embedding-001` embeddings, combined with Postgres full-text `TSVECTOR` keyword
search — over content ingested from the customer's own PDFs, DOCX, TXT and crawled site.
An optional relevance gate declines to answer when nothing relevant is retrieved, rather
than improvising. **[T1/T2]** — `rag_service.py`, `embedder.py`, root `CLAUDE.md` RAG
pipeline section.

**SalesIQ.** Ships Zobot, a bot builder with multiple authoring modes (a codeless flow
builder, a scripting option, and AI-assisted answering), plus a resource/article library
the bot can draw from. Reaching a well-grounded AI answering experience is a build,
configured by the customer. **[CAT]**

**The honest version of our edge:** not "they can't do RAG" — it is that our grounded
answering is the default state of a new account rather than the output of a bot-building
project. Frame it as *time-to-a-good-answer*, not capability absence.

### 3.2 Built-in sales qualification — *edge: oye (strongest axis)*

**OyeChats.** Four frameworks selectable per bot — BANT, MEDDIC, CHAMP, GPCTBA+C&I —
confirmed in `PRESET_FRAMEWORKS`. Weighted dimensions produce a normalised composite
score 0–100, mapping to tiers `unqualified` → `mql` (≥30) → `sal` (≥55) → `sql` (≥75).
Extraction runs fire-and-forget *after* the answer has streamed, so it never slows the
visitor down, and it infers signals from natural conversation rather than interrogating.
Interactive qualification chips ship **off by default on every dimension of every
framework** (code comment `BR-04`). Signals are written append-only to `BANTSignal`, and
scoring never downgrades. Crossing into SQL fires an email notification and a
`tier_transition` webhook. **[T1]** — `qualification_service.py`, `rag_service.py`,
`docs/notebooklm/features/OYECHATS_FEATURE_QUALIFICATION.md`.

**SalesIQ.** Has visitor scoring and lead-ranking concepts oriented around engagement and
visitor behaviour, and rich visitor tracking to feed them. It does not present a named
B2B sales-qualification framework — BANT, MEDDIC, CHAMP, GPCTBA+C&I — as a configurable
product primitive. **[CAT]**

**This is the sharpest, most defensible claim on the page.** It is specific, it is
verifiable in our own code, and it is a genuine category difference: behavioural visitor
scoring answers "who is engaged?", framework qualification answers "who is ready to buy,
and on which dimension are they still weak?". Lead with it.

### 3.3 Live human handoff — *edge: tie*

**OyeChats.** An explicit code-level state machine (`session_state_machine.py`) with four
states (`bot`, `waiting`, `live`, `closed`) and enforced transitions, operator routing,
departments, canned `/shortcut` responses, offline message capture, an immutable
`ChatAuditLog`, and WebSocket delivery. The operator inherits the full transcript and the
qualification score. **[T1]** — `docs/notebooklm/features/OYECHATS_FEATURE_LIVE_CHAT.md`.

**SalesIQ.** Live chat is their core competency and it is mature: routing, departments,
canned replies, operator mobile apps, and richer in-chat modalities than we offer. **[CAT]**

**Call this a tie, and mean it.** Claiming an edge on live chat against a dedicated live
chat vendor is the fastest way to lose a reader's trust on the rest of the page. Our
*specific* advantage here is narrow and should be stated narrowly: the handoff carries a
qualification score, so the operator knows the lead's tier before they type. That is a
consequence of §3.2, not a live-chat win.

### 3.4 Analytics — *edge: tie*

**OyeChats.** Conversation, lead and qualification analytics; visitor events and UTM
capture; `BotGrowthEvent` per-bot business events. Our analytics answer sales questions.
**[T2]**

**SalesIQ.** Long-established visitor-tracking and engagement analytics, and inside the
Zoho suite those reports flow into a much larger reporting surface than ours. **[CAT]**

Tie. Different questions answered, neither strictly larger.

### 3.5 Integrations and CRM posture — *edge: oye for non-Zoho buyers, them for Zoho buyers*

**OyeChats.** Outbound webhooks with HMAC signing and a 5-attempt retry ladder
(30s / 2m / 10m / 1h / 4h), a REST API with a full OpenAPI spec, and script-tag installs
documented for HTML, Next.js, React, Vue, Angular, Shopify, Squarespace, Svelte, Webflow,
Wix, WordPress, Framer, Bubble, Astro and GTM. **[T1/T2]** — `webhook_service.py`,
`platformLogos.ts`, website `integrations.tsx`.

**SalesIQ.** Native, first-party, deeply-wired integration with Zoho CRM, Zoho Desk and
the wider suite — the kind of integration that only the vendor who owns both sides can
build. **[CAT]**

**State our gap explicitly on the page: we have no native Zoho CRM integration.** Leads
reach any CRM through webhooks and the REST API, which is genuinely CRM-agnostic and
genuinely more work than a first-party connector. Both halves of that sentence are true
and both belong on the page. Pretending otherwise gets found out in the first demo.

### 3.6 Time to go live — *edge: oye*

Add content, paste one script tag. The customer-facing loader is ~3KB gzipped, mounts into
a shadow root so host-page CSS cannot break it, and lazy-loads the React app only when a
visitor actually opens the widget. **[T2]** — root `CLAUDE.md` widget-embedding section.

Frame as *setup depth*, not vendor incompetence: a suite product with visitor tracking,
routing rules, departments and a bot builder is configured before it is useful, and that
is the cost of its flexibility.

### 3.7 Pricing model — *edge: them on headline price, oye on how it scales*

**OyeChats — our own numbers, safe to publish, already live on `/pricing`. [T2]**

| Plan | Monthly INR | Monthly USD | Credits/mo | Seats |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free | ₹0 | $0 | 100 | 1 |
| Starter | ₹599 | $7.99 | 1,000 | 1 |
| Standard | ₹1,199 | $15.99 | 2,500 | 2 |
| Professional | ₹2,999 | $45.99 | 8,000 | 3 |

Extra operator seat ₹499 / $5 per month. Annual saves ~20%. Credits: 1 per AI reply,
5 per URL scan, 10 per email verification, 5 per company lookup, 1 per 250 words of
document scanning; operator messages and system emails cost nothing. Plan credits reset
monthly, top-up credits never expire and burn FIFO. **[T2]** — `src/lib/pricing.ts`.

> **GST.** Every published price is GST-**exclusive**. For an Indian customer, tax is
> added at charge time (`api/app/core/tax.py::gross_charge_minor`) — ₹1,199 listed is
> ₹1,414.82 debited. For an international customer the sale is an export of services and
> no Indian GST applies. **If the page shows any all-in figure, it must show this.** An
> Indian SMB comparing an inclusive rival quote against our exclusive one is being misled
> by our own page, and that is the exact mistake this note exists to prevent. **[T2]**

**SalesIQ.** Per-operator tiers with a free entry tier, discounted annually, and best
value when bought as part of the wider Zoho suite rather than standalone. **[CAT]**

**The honest verdict, and it must be honest:** on a straight headline comparison for a
small team, SalesIQ is very aggressively priced and frequently the cheaper line item —
particularly for a business already inside the Zoho suite, where the marginal cost is
close to nothing. Mark this axis `them` in the matrix, exactly as the current entry
already does. Our pricing argument is not "cheaper", it is **shape**: our cost scales
with conversation volume, not with headcount, so adding a fourth person who wants to see
the inbox does not re-price the product. Say that, and only that.

### 3.8 Data residency and India posture — *edge: them, and say so*

**[VERIFY-BEFORE-PUBLISH]** Zoho operates region-specific data centres including India,
and markets Indian data residency. This is a real advantage for Indian buyers with
residency requirements and it is not something we should try to talk past. Our
infrastructure posture (DigitalOcean, Cloudflare R2, third-party LLM providers) needs to
be stated accurately by someone who checks the current deployment before this section
ships. **Do not publish this section until that check is done and dated.**

### 3.9 Ecosystem breadth — *edge: them*

Zoho One spans dozens of applications. We are one product. A buyer who wants CRM, desk,
books, campaigns, HR and chat on one bill should buy Zoho, and the page should say that
plainly in the "choose them instead" section rather than burying it.

### 3.10 Model and vendor independence — *edge: oye*

We route through LiteLLM with an explicit primary/fallback pair (OpenAI `gpt-5.4-mini`
→ Google `gemini-2.5-flash`), so the answering model is a configuration choice and a
provider outage degrades rather than stops the product. **[T2]** — `llm_service.py`,
root `CLAUDE.md`. Frame as resilience and independence. Do **not** turn this into a claim
about answer quality versus Zia — we have not benchmarked that. **[DO NOT PUBLISH]**

## 4. The "choose Zoho SalesIQ instead" section

Written to be genuinely useful. Ship close to verbatim:

> **Choose Zoho SalesIQ if you already run on Zoho.** If your CRM is Zoho CRM and your
> helpdesk is Zoho Desk, SalesIQ plugs into both natively, in a way no third-party tool
> can match. Your chats land next to your deals with no webhook plumbing, your team is
> already inside the suite, and the marginal cost of adding chat is small. Choose it too
> if what you need is primarily a strong human live chat product with deep visitor
> tracking and you want the AI layer to be something you configure over time rather than
> the thing you start with, or if Indian data residency inside a suite you already trust
> is a requirement rather than a preference.
>
> **Choose OyeChats if you are not on Zoho, or if qualification is the point.** If you
> want an AI agent that answers from your own content on day one and hands your team a
> BANT- or MEDDIC-scored lead rather than a transcript, that is what OyeChats is built to
> be — and it pushes those leads to whatever CRM you use.
