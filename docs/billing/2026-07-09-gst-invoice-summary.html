1Conclusion

Where the system stands, in plain terms.

Domestic (Indian, INR) invoicing is production-grade and above the industry norm. Gapless per-financial-year numbering (IST), immutable seller/buyer snapshots, exact single-rounding-point tax math, correct credit-note reversals, and a live reconciliation surface. This path can be signed off.
Cross-border / USD billing is not yet safe to switch on. An “international” sale settled in INR is not an export for GST — treating it as zero-rated (no tax) without foreign-exchange realisation (FIRC) + a valid LUT is the one mistake that turns a clean system into a liability. It is correctly fenced off today (INTL_PAYMENTS_ENABLED=false → “contact sales”).
  • GST is inclusive. The customer pays the sticker price; you do not add tax on top. The invoice discloses the GST already embedded in the price.
  • The embedded GST is real money you owe the government — roughly 15.25% of every rupee collected (18% inclusive). It is not “just for the invoice.” Your true revenue = sticker ÷ 1.18.
  • India = GST; genuine export = zero-rated — but the test is place of supply + forex, never citizenship or physical location. Today, 100% of real (INR) sales are domestic → GST on all of them.

4Indian vs foreign customers — how GST is decided

The legal test is place of supply + foreign-exchange realisation — never citizenship, and never physical location alone.

🇮🇳 Indian customer GST applies

Billing country = IN, or paid by UPI / Indian card, or a valid Indian GSTIN.

  • Domestic supply → 18% GST, inclusive, in INR.
  • Same state as seller → CGST + SGST. Different state → IGST.
  • App shows INR; Razorpay charges the sticker.

🌐 Foreign customer export / blocked today

Billing country ≠ IN → app shows USD; paid checkout is blocked → “contact sales”.

  • A genuine export is zero-rated (no GST) — only if forex is received (FIRC) + valid LUT + recipient truly outside India.
  • An INR-settled “foreign” sale is not an export → GST still due.
  • Not enabled until the USD/forex rail ships (Phase 2).

The tricky cases — answered

ScenarioGST?Why
Indian resident, Indian account, pays from the USA (travelling)YES · INRRecipient is in India → domestic supply. Physical location doesn’t relocate the recipient. IP mismatch is logged as a FEMA signal, not blocked.
US citizen resident in India, US card, pays from IndiaYES · INRGST ignores citizenship. Recipient is in India → GST. A single foreign card cannot waive it.
Indian company with GSTIN buyingYES · INRB2B domestic. GSTIN pins the state (place of supply) and enables the buyer’s Input Tax Credit.
Genuine non-resident abroad, foreign card, forex received, LUT validNO · exportRecipient outside India + payment in forex → zero-rated export. Citizenship irrelevant.
Indian resident on a trip — selects a foreign country in billing & pays from a foreign bank in USDDEPENDS · forexThe dropdown doesn’t decide it — forex settlement does. Money received as INR (no FIRC) → not an export → GST due. Genuinely received as USD forex + FIRC with a valid LUT → defensible zero-rated export (you rely on consistent proxies + forex, not on detecting residency). Travelling doesn’t relocate residence, but a good-faith proxy+forex determination is what the OIDAR rules ask of you.
Someone in India selecting “USA” to dodge taxYES — you’d owe itNot a real export. If you skip GST, OyeChats pays it out of pocket + penalty. Hence the two-proxy + forex gate.
How OyeChats decides without documents: GST for a digital (OIDAR) service is designed around proxies you already have — billing country/state, card issuer country (UPI = always India), and IP. Rule: if ≥2 proxies point to India → charge GST. Treat as export only when ≥2 point abroad and forex is realised (FIRC) and a valid LUT is on file. When signals conflict, default to charging GST (supplier-safe). No citizenship or paperwork required.

Optional safeguard: a checkout confirmation — “I confirm I am billed outside India and not an Indian resident” — cleanly shifts the misrepresentation risk onto the customer for the travelling-Indian case above. And the hard backstop is always forex settlement: an INR-settled sale can never be a zero-rated export, whatever the customer selected.

7What to do

Now — domestic hygiene

  • F9 — confirm the Razorpay ↔ seller PAN/GSTIN entity chain.
  • F5 — stand up monthly RCM on imported SaaS.
  • F4 — add a recipient-state field at checkout.
  • F7 — add the 30-Nov credit-note guard.

Before enabling USD (hard gate)

  • F2 — forex-evidence-gated zero-rating + a forex-settling rail + FIRC capture.

  • F10 — LUT validity window + expiry alerts.
  • Extend finalize to produce a statutory INR value for non-INR supplies.
